# Prehistoric Chronology — Scientific Reference

> **Status:** English scientific reference synthesis for an educational visualisation project — **the editorial source of record** for chronological milestones mirrored into `app/data/events.json`. This document is not a peer-reviewed publication or an authoritative taxonomic database. It is designed to make the evidence, uncertainty and competing interpretations behind the visual application legible to readers and reviewers. It is not a literal translation of any prior working file.
>
> **Review rule:** a claim in this file should be checked against the cited primary publication. Do not strengthen, simplify, translate away, or silently drop its epistemic qualification when adapting it for application data or visual display.
>
> **Maintenance:** keep this filename stable (no year stamp). When new papers appear, update this document in place and record the review date below — then check whether `app/data/events.json` (and embedded mirrors) need a corresponding change.
>
> **Last reviewed:** 28–29 August 2026

## How to read this document

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Date** | Best-supported current estimate, usually expressed as a range or with its published uncertainty |
| **Status** | Relationship to the educational timeline: confirmed, revised, new, or actively debated |
| **Evidence** | Main evidential basis: fossil morphology, archaeology, geochronology, ancient DNA, proteomics, or inference |
| **Debate** | Whether competing interpretations affect taxonomy, chronology, phylogeny or behavioural inference |
| **DOI** | Persistent identifier for the primary source; verify title, authors, year and corrigenda before relying on it |

**Important distinction:** direct observations and measurements are not equivalent to reconstructions. A well-dated fossil may still have a disputed taxonomic position; a taxon may be accepted while its relationship to later populations remains unresolved.

## Misconceptions the visual application should correct

| Misconception | Correct framing |
|---|---|
| “Human evolution was a ladder.” | Hominin evolution is branching, with coexistence, extinction, incomplete sampling and unresolved relationships. A chronological sequence is not a direct ancestor–descendant sequence. |
| “A fossil taxon is automatically a settled species.” | The validity of a taxon, the attribution of a specimen and the taxon’s phylogenetic position are separate questions. |
| “Four hominin lineages were identified at Ledi-Geraru.” | The 2025 study concludes at least three lineages in the wider Afar region between 3.0 and 2.5 Ma. The Ledi-Geraru dental sample documents two taxa. |
| “*Paranthropus* was found in the Afar.” | Villmoare et al. explicitly report no *Paranthropus* fossils from the Afar region to date. |
| “Little Foot is a third established *Australopithecus* species.” | Martin et al. reject attribution of StW 573 to *A. prometheus*; that negative conclusion does not formally name a new species. Taxonomy and dating remain separate debates. |
| “*Homo juluensis* is a newly discovered human species.” | It is a recent taxonomic proposal for fossils known for decades, not a new field discovery. Its relation to Denisovans is hypothetical. |
| “The Chinese-fossil debate is China versus the West.” | Researchers from Chinese institutions hold different views, and international co-authorship crosses national lines. The disagreement is scientific, not geopolitical. |
| “Thomas Quarry I proves the common ancestor of all later human lineages.” | The date is robust; the precise phylogenetic interpretation remains more tentative and has alternatives. |
| “A reconstructed silhouette shows known skin, hair and eye colour.” | Fossils, genetics and graphic reconstruction provide different kinds of evidence. Unknown or indirectly inferred traits should be visually and textually marked as such. |

## Editorial review checklist

Before changing a public-facing scientific claim in the application:

1. Read the relevant entry in this document and the corresponding primary publication.
2. Check the DOI, title, authors, journal, publication year and any correction or editorial notice.
3. State the evidence type: fossil observation, osteological estimate, geochronology, ancient DNA, proteomics, comparative morphology, phylogenetic inference or visual reconstruction.
4. Separate direct evidence from inference and distinguish uncertainty from absence of evidence.
5. Record competing interpretations separately from the factual observation.
6. Update the human-readable scientific reference first.
7. Update the corresponding structured data in `app/data/` without dropping DOI, evidence or uncertainty information.
8. Run `python scripts/sync_embedded.py` and verify the public interface.
9. Open a reviewable pull request that identifies the changed claim, source, evidence type, alternatives and confidence level.

## Reference note

This file is intended to be maintained alongside the application. It should not be cited as a substitute for the primary literature. It is a traceable editorial aid for an educational visualisation whose central goal is to show that the evidence for human evolution is rich, cumulative and often disputed—not a finished, linear story.
