## Reading convention
| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ Direct DNA | Pigmentation data derived from ancient DNA or sequenced genomes |
| 🔬 Indirect genetics | Inference via molecular selection signal (e.g. MC1R sweep) without a direct sequence |
| ⚠️ Evolutionary inference | Phylogenetic reasoning, no DNA available |
| ❓ Open debate | Contradictory results between research teams, unresolved |

***
## 0a. *Sahelanthropus tchadensis* (Toumaï) — ~7 million years
<!-- catalogue-id: sahelanthropus -->
**Phylogeny:** Oldest known pre-human, discovered in 2001 at Toros-Menalla (Djourab Desert, Chad) by the French–Chadian MPFT mission led by Michel Brunet.[^T1]

**Biometrics:** cranial volume 320–380 cm³; estimated height ~110–120 cm; estimated weight ~35 kg (inferred from femur TM 266-01-063).

**Morphology:** short, flat face (rare for this antiquity), very pronounced brow ridges, canines reduced compared to contemporary great apes. The centred foramen magnum suggests habitual bipedalism, but the position of the later-discovered femur remains contested (Macchiarelli et al. 2020).[^T1][^T2]

**Pigmentation ⚠️ Evolutionary inference:** No retrievable DNA at this depth. Population probably fully fur-covered, with lightly pigmented underlying skin, as in living chimpanzees.

**Debate:** Truly bipedal hominin status contested (Wolpoff et al. 2002; Macchiarelli et al. 2020). Exact phylogenetic position undecided — pre-hominin or simple Miocene great ape?

***
## 0b. *Ardipithecus ramidus* — ~4.4 million years
<!-- catalogue-id: ardipithecus -->
**Phylogeny:** Forest-dwelling hominin described in 2009 by Tim White et al. "Ardi" skeleton (ARA-VP-6/500) from Aramis (Middle Awash, Ethiopia). Preceded by *Ardipithecus kadabba* (5.8–5.2 Ma).[^T3]

**Biometrics:** female height ~120 cm; female weight ~50 kg; cranial volume 300–350 cm³.

**Morphology:** remarkable evolutionary mosaic: short, broad pelvis adapted to bipedalism but a divergent, opposable big toe permitting climbing. Long arms, long flexible fingers. Reduced sexual dimorphism in canines (suggesting less competitive sociality than chimpanzees). Lived in a wooded habitat, contradicting the savanna hypothesis for the origin of bipedalism.

**Pigmentation ⚠️ Evolutionary inference:** Same logic as for Toumaï — full pelage, lightly pigmented underlying skin.

**Debate:** Actual degree of bipedalism disputed (Sarmiento 2010); placement within the human lineage not universally accepted.

***
## 1. *Australopithecus afarensis* — 4.1 to 2.7 million years
<!-- catalogue-id: afarensis -->
**Phylogeny:** Pre-*Homo* hominid, probable ancestor of the genus *Homo*. Notably described via the "Lucy" skeleton (AL 288-1, Hadar, Ethiopia).[^1]

**Biometrics:** male height ~150 cm; female height ~105–110 cm; male weight ~42 kg; female weight ~29 kg; brain volume 385–550 cm³; marked sexual dimorphism (~40%).

**Morphology:** habitual biped retaining substantial arboreal capacity. Very prognathic face; conical rib cage as in great apes; arched feet; proportionally long arms. No chin.

**Pigmentation ⚠️ Evolutionary inference:** No retrievable DNA at this depth. Early hominins were probably fur-covered with lightly pigmented underlying skin, as in living chimpanzees whose unexposed skin is light. Selection toward dark skin begins after loss of body hair, estimated between 1.2 and 2 million years ago.[^2][^3]

**Debate:** Bipedalism vs. arboreality (Lovejoy vs. Stern & Susman); timing and mechanism of pelage loss debated.

***
## 2. *Homo habilis* — 2.4 to 1.4 million years
<!-- catalogue-id: habilis -->
**Phylogeny:** First recognised member of the genus *Homo*; contemporary with *Australopithecus*. Coherent species status still debated (Wood & Collard 1999).[^4]

**Biometrics:** male height ~130–135 cm; female height ~100–120 cm; male weight ~37 kg; brain volume 500–900 cm³.

**Morphology:** rounder skull than australopiths; reduced brow ridge; dentition closer to modern humans. Proportionally long arms; mixed locomotion. Associated with the Oldowan industry (Olduvai Gorge).

**Pigmentation ⚠️ Evolutionary inference:** Transitional period: partial body pelage → emerging dark skin. Loss of pelage exposes skin to African UV, driving strong selection toward melanisation.[^2][^3]

***
## 3. *Homo erectus* — 1.8 million to 250,000 years
<!-- catalogue-id: erectus -->
**Phylogeny:** First hominin with body proportions genuinely comparable to modern humans. Present in Africa, Europe and Asia. The "Turkana Boy" specimen (KNM-WT 15000) is one of the most complete skeletons in the entire human evolutionary record.[^5]

**Biometrics:** male height 145–185 cm; female height 130–160 cm; male weight 40–68 kg; brain volume 600–1,100 cm³ (mean ~900 cm³).

**Morphology:** low, elongated skull, very pronounced brow ridge (double supraorbital torus), sloping forehead, no chin. Slender body adapted for running; long lower limbs; barrel-shaped rib cage. Marked geographic variation: more gracile African forms, more robust Asian forms.

**Pigmentation 🔬 Indirect genetics:** The transition to **dark skin** in *Homo erectus* is supported by molecular analysis of the *MC1R* gene: a phylogenetic study dates the **selective sweep** toward strong pigmentation to **~1.2 million years ago**, coinciding with the loss of body pelage documented for the same period.[^3]

**Debate:** *H. erectus sensu lato* is probably a polyphyletic grouping (Rogers et al. 2020); substantial regional variation.

***
## 3b. *Homo erectus georgicus* (Dmanisi) — ~1.77 million years
<!-- catalogue-id: georgicus -->
**Phylogeny:** Archaic *Homo erectus* population found at Dmanisi (Georgia). Oldest known hominins outside Africa. Five skulls described by Lordkipanidze et al. (2013) show exceptionally wide morphological variability within a single synchronous population.[^T4]

**Biometrics:** male height ~145–166 cm; male weight ~40–50 kg; cranial volume 546–775 cm³ (skull D4500: 546 cm³).

**Morphology:** skull D4500 ("Skull 5"): a very small neurocranium combined with a massive, prognathic face — an unmatched combination. Gracile stature, modern-like limb proportions. No chin, brow ridge marked but variable.

**Debate:** The high intra-population variability leads Lordkipanidze to suggest *H. ergaster*, *H. erectus* and *H. habilis* might belong to a single polymorphic species ("single-species hypothesis"). This hypothesis is not widely accepted — most palaeoanthropologists retain the habilis/erectus separation.

***
## 4. *Homo heidelbergensis* — 700,000 to 200,000 years
<!-- catalogue-id: heidelbergensis -->
**Phylogeny:** Probable ancestor of the Neanderthals in Eurasia and possibly of *H. sapiens* in Africa. Coherent species status heavily debated. The Thomas Quarry I fossils (Casablanca, 773,000 years) fit within the African lineage leading to this species.[^T6]

**Biometrics:** male height ~175 cm (Kabwe individuals up to 181 cm); female height ~157 cm; brain volume 1,100–1,400 cm³.

**Pigmentation ⚠️ Evolutionary inference:** No retrievable DNA. Light-skin alleles (*SLC24A5*, *SLC45A2*, *TYRP1*) only appear much later (11,000–19,000 years before present). African evolutionary continuity and UV exposure conditions strongly favour dark skin.[^6]

***
## 5. *Homo neanderthalensis* — 400,000 to 28,000 years
<!-- catalogue-id: neanderthal -->
**Phylogeny:** Sister species of *H. sapiens*; both species evolved from *H. heidelbergensis* / a common African ancestor. 2–4% Neanderthal DNA introgression persists in the genomes of present-day non-African humans.[^7][^8]

**Biometrics:** male height ~164 cm (range 148–177 cm); female height ~155 cm; male weight ~65 kg; brain volume 1,200–1,750 cm³ (slightly greater than average *H. sapiens*).

**Morphology:** elongated skull with a characteristic occipital "bun"; strong continuous brow ridge; very projecting midface; wide nasal aperture. Short limbs (cold adaptation, Allen's rule). Wide rib cage from birth, confirmed by a study on Neanderthal development (*Science Advances*, 2020). Hypertrophied musculature.

**Pigmentation ✅ Direct DNA:** the first species for which pigmentation is **partially documented by DNA**.

*MC1R gene (Lalueza-Fox et al., 2007, *Science*):* sequencing of the MC1R gene in two specimens (El Sidrón, Spain; Monti Lessini, Italy) identified a Neanderthal-specific mutation (R307G) reducing MC1R activity — a mechanism similar to the variants producing pale skin and red hair in modern humans. Estimated to occur in ~1% of individuals.[^9]

*Complete genomes (Vindija, Croatia; 3 females):* analysis of pigmentation loci suggests dark-brown skin, brown eyes, brown-chestnut hair (Cerqueira 2012). Results methodologically disputed.

*OCA2 introgression:* a Neanderthal haplotype carrying an *OCA2* allele associated with blue eyes was introgressed into *H. sapiens* and came under positive selection in some Eurasian populations — implying some Neanderthals carried blue-eye variants.[^10]

**Debate:** Hawks (2023) highlights the methodological limits of phenotypic inference from Neanderthal genomes. Neanderthal → *H. sapiens* pigmentation introgression remains debated (Iasi et al., *Science*, 2024).

***
## 5b. *Homo antecessor* — ~1.2 million to 770,000 years
<!-- catalogue-id: antecessor -->
**Phylogeny:** Discovered 1994–1997 at Atapuerca (Spain; Sima del Elefante and Gran Dolina TD6). Long considered a candidate for the common ancestor of Neanderthals and *Sapiens*. This interpretation is **revised in 2026** by Hublin et al. in light of the Thomas Quarry I fossils (Morocco, 773 ka), which now occupy this basal position.[^T5][^T6]

**Debate:** phylogenetic status entirely rethought in 2026: a sister population (not an ancestor) to the clade leading to the Thomas Quarry I hominins and beyond. The modern-looking facial traits would represent morphological convergence.

***
## 6. Denisovans — ~400,000 to ~30,000 years
<!-- catalogue-id: denisovan -->
**Phylogeny:** Sister species of the Neanderthals; 3–5% Denisovan DNA persists in present-day Asian and Oceanian populations. Until 2025, known only from tiny fragments (a finger bone, teeth). In **June 2025**, Fu Qiaomei et al. (*Science* and *Cell*, Beijing) identified the "Dragon Man" skull (Harbin, China, ~146,000 years) as Denisovan via ancient proteomics and mitochondrial DNA.[^11][^12][^13][^14]

**Biometrics:** estimated male height >180 cm; estimated lean mass ~100 kg (Bence Viola, University of Toronto, 2025); brain volume high, estimated ≥ *H. sapiens*.

**Morphology (Dragon Man, 2025):** large, massive skull; prominent brow ridges; rounded nose; flat cheekbones; large mouth; unusually large molars, distinct from Neanderthals. The Xiahe (Tibet) and Penghu (Taiwan) jaws: very robust, chinless.

**Pigmentation ✅ Direct DNA:** dark skin, dark eyes, dark hair — all directly supported by ancient DNA.[^12]

**Debate:** Dragon Man = Denisovan identification confirmed by proteomics + mtDNA (2025); nuclear DNA is not yet available — several palaeoanthropologists await this confirmation before concluding definitively.

***
## 6b. *Homo longi* / *Homo juluensis* — Chinese Middle Pleistocene — ~1 million to ~50,000 years
<!-- catalogue-id: longi -->
> **Taxonomic status: ❓ Open debate (ACTIVE_DEBATE)** — three competing positions, all defended by established research teams. None commands consensus in 2026.

**Phylogeny:** The classification of Middle Pleistocene *Homo* in East Asia is traditionally called "the muddle in the middle." Two rival proposals now structure the debate. In 2021, Ni Xijun et al. described the Harbin skull as *Homo longi*. In November 2024, Bae Christopher J. (University of Hawai'i) and Wu Xiujie (Chinese Academy of Sciences) proposed a distinct taxon, *Homo juluensis* ("big head"), grouping Xujiayao (holotype), Xuchang (paratype), Penghu 1 and possibly the Denisovans.[^HJ1] In September 2025, the digital reconstruction of the Yunxian 2 skull (~1 Ma) placed this fossil as an early member of an Asian *H. longi* clade that includes the Denisovans.[^HJ2]

### The three competing positions
| Position | Proponents | Central argument | What is missing to settle it |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Homo juluensis* = distinct species | Bae C.J. & Wu X. (Univ. of Hawaiʻi / Chinese Academy of Sciences), *Nature Communications*, 2024[^HJ1] | Low, wide skull, brain volume >1,000 cm³, reduced and inward-turned mastoid, parietal depression, Neanderthal-like bony labyrinth, large teeth, shoveled incisors, wide mandibular ramus | No nuclear DNA for the Xujiayao and Xuchang fossils; the article underwent an editorial correction one month after publication[^HJ3] |
| *Homo longi* suffices (lumping) | Stringer C. (Natural History Museum, London), co-author of Feng et al. 2025[^HJ2] | Most of these fossils (Yunxian, Dali, Jinniushan, Hualongdong, Harbin) form a single *H. longi* clade; the name *longi* has nomenclatural priority (2021) | Phylogenetic position rests on morphometrics, not genomics |
| Caution — insufficient data | Ni X. (Chinese Academy of Sciences), author of the original *H. longi* description | Inter-site variability and lack of nuclear DNA do not allow formal validation of an additional taxon | — |

> **Why this conflict is instructive:** the author who named *Homo longi* in 2021 (Ni Xijun) is the same person urging caution about proliferating new species names. Ni and Stringer — from different scientific traditions — co-author the 2025 Yunxian 2 study together.[^HJ2] This is not a "China vs. the West" dispute, contrary to what some popular science content suggests.

### Misconception to flag
Several popularisation sources present *Homo juluensis* as a "newly discovered human species," or even as a newly unearthed fossil. This is inaccurate on two counts: the fossils concerned have been known for decades (Xujiayao was excavated in the 1970s), and this is a taxonomic reclassification proposal, not a field discovery. Likewise, the equivalence "*H. juluensis* = the Denisovans" is a hypothesis by Bae & Wu, not an established result.

**Debate:** active and unresolved. The bottleneck is identical across all three positions: absence of nuclear DNA for the Chinese fossils other than Harbin.

## 7. *Homo sapiens* — Early Africa — 315,000 to 100,000 years
<!-- catalogue-id: sapiens-africa -->
**Phylogeny:** Jebel Irhoud fossils (315,000 years): a mosaic of modern (flat face) and archaic (elongated neurocranium) traits. Omo Kibish (195,000 years, revised to 233,000) and Herto (160,000 years) fossils: closer to present-day humans.[^15][^16]

**Pigmentation ✅ Direct ancient DNA:** *Nature* (2025, Jakobsson & Schlebusch, Uppsala/Cape Town): 28 ancient genomes from southern Africa (10,200 to 150 years BP) show that alleles for strong pigmentation, brown eyes, and lactose non-persistence are fixed throughout the ancestral population — dark skin is the robustly supported ancestral condition for *H. sapiens*, not an assumption.

## Main active scientific conflicts (2026)

**1. Neanderthal pigmentation.** Lalueza-Fox (*Science*, 2007) found a unique MC1R mutation implying possible pale skin and red hair in ~1% of individuals. Cerqueira (2012), using complete genomes, found a majority with dark-brown skin and brown eyes. Hawks (2023) notes both analyses have limitations; the question is not definitively settled. Iasi et al. (*Science*, 2024) confirm Neanderthal-to-*H. sapiens* introgression of pigmentation alleles, but the frequencies involved remain debated.

**2. Decoupling of light eyes and light skin in Europe.** Light eyes appear several millennia **before** light skin. The mechanism (sexual selection? genetic drift? pleiotropy with another adaptive trait?) remains unresolved.

**3. Is Dragon Man a Denisovan?** Proteomics and mtDNA confirm Denisovan affiliation (Fu Qiaomei et al., 2025). Several teams are awaiting nuclear DNA before concluding definitively.

**4. Timing of skin lightening in Europe.** Ghirotto et al. (*PNAS*, 2025) and Ju & Kelleher (*PNAS*, 2021) agree the process is recent (~3,000 years) but disagree on the precise tempo of positive selection vs. drift vs. migration.

**5. Is *Homo juluensis* a valid species or an over-split grouping?** Bae & Wu (*Nature Communications*, 2024) defend a new taxon on morphological grounds. Stringer prefers to lump most of these fossils under the earlier name *H. longi*. Ni Xijun — himself the author of the original *H. longi* description — considers the data insufficient to validate an additional taxon. The Yunxian 2 reconstruction (Feng et al., *Science*, 2025, co-signed by Stringer and Ni) strengthens the case for a single, deep *H. longi* clade without closing the debate. See section 6b.

**6. Thomas Quarry I: basal population or diverging *H. erectus*?** Hublin et al. (*Nature*, 2026) describe the ~773,000-year-old Moroccan hominins as basal to the *H. sapiens* lineage. The Brunhes/Matuyama dating is solid. The phylogenetic interpretation, however, allows an alternative: this could be an *Homo erectus* population undergoing isolation and divergence rather than a strictly basal population for the three lineages. This nuance is rarely reflected in press summaries, which often present the result as definitive. Status to retain: moderate consensus on interpretation, strong consensus on dating.
